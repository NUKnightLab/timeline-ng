Getting Started

What is a timeline?

A timeline tells a story as a sequence of dated slides. People move through a timeline either by reading slide-to-slide with the Next/Previous controls, or by scrubbing the navigation strip along the bottom of the screen to jump straight to a date. We have tried to make them really easy to read and to make, but it never hurts to explain the details.

The two halves of the screen

Every timeline splits into two regions: a big slide viewer showing the current slide's content, and a thin TimeNav strip below it showing where that slide sits in time relative to everything else.

The orange dot is the slide currently shown above; the gray dots are the other slides in the timeline.

Clicking on a marker or label in the TimeNav brings the slide for that event into view. By default, the TimeNav is drawn to show the entire span of the story. Sometimes that means that labels overlap and are hard to read. Viewers can zoom in, and the labels will spread out. Viewers can also drag the handle at the center top of the TimeNav to show more or fewer rows. At the smallest, no labels are visible, only event markers along the axis line.

Anatomy of a slide

Each slide is built from the same handful of pieces. Any of them can be left out except the date (title slides are the one case where you don't use a date — see below).

The date is required on every slide except the optional title slide. Everything else is optional.

  • Date — when the event happened. One date makes a point; adding an end date makes it a range, shown as a span on the TimeNav. The start and end date can be just years, or can be more specific as appropriate.
  • Headline — a short title for the slide. Can include bold and italic, and other basic HTML.
  • Text — the body of the slide. Also allows basic HTML, including links.
  • Media — an image, video, audio clip, or embed illustrating the event, with an optional caption and credit line. See media types for more details.
  • Background — an optional color or image behind the slide itself.
  • Group — an optional label used to sort related slides into their own row on the TimeNav (see below).

You can also change whether the media is on the left or right to add some variety to your slides.

The title slide

A timeline can optionally start with one special slide that has no date — it's just an introduction to the whole story, shown before the first event. It's built from the same headline / text / media pieces as any other slide, it just isn't placed on TimeNav.

Grouping events

Slides that share the same group label get their own row on the TimeNav instead of all sharing one line. This is useful for stories with multiple parallel threads — say, tracking two people's timelines side by side, or separating "cultural" events from "political" ones.

Three groups ("Politics", "Culture", "Science") each get their own labeled row.

Slides without a group value all share a single unlabeled row. If the handle is used to make the TimeNav smaller than enough to fit all groups, the labels will be hidden and the event labels may no longer be aligned by the group.
