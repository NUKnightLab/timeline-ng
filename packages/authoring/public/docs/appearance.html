Appearance

Typeface, navigator and contrast are three separate choices

Three settings control how a timeline looks, and they are deliberately independent — each answers one question, and any combination is valid. They are all available in the settings panel next to the preview, and all three can also be set directly in a timeline's settings block.

Typeface

fontPairing chooses a headline face and a body face together. These pairings are carried over from the classic TimelineJS, so a timeline moved across should be able to keep the look it had.

The Scripts column matters if your timeline is not written in the Latin alphabet. Most of these families cover Latin and sometimes Cyrillic and little else. A timeline in a writing system the faces don't cover still displays perfectly well — the text falls back to a standard system font, character by character — but it won't look like the pairing you picked. The settings panel warns you when your timeline's language falls outside the pairing's coverage.

Georgia + Helvetica Neue is the default and the one exception: it uses fonts already on the reader's device, so it downloads nothing and covers every writing system. It is the safe choice rather than the dull one.

Navigator

navChrome controls how much of the timeline navigator — the strip along the bottom — is shown.

SettingShows
"standard" The full navigator: event markers and labels, the date axis, zoom controls, and a bar showing which part of the timeline you are looking at. This is the default.
"minimal" Just the track, the markers and their labels. The zoom controls, date axis and position bar are removed, and the navigator gets shorter, leaving more room for your slides.

If your events bunch together — many in one decade, or one ancient event and the rest recent — the navigator cannot label all of them without the labels colliding. It shows as many as fit, always including the event you are currently viewing, and leaves the rest as markers on the track. Those markers still work: click one to jump to it. Zooming in spreads the events out and the labels reappear.

High contrast

highContrast raises every piece of text and every marker to the strictest contrast level in the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG AAA), uses heavier type, and thickens the outline that shows which element has keyboard focus.

It applies on top of whichever typeface and navigator setting you have chosen, and it takes precedence over both wherever they disagree about a colour.

Turning this off does not mean your timeline is inaccessible. The normal appearance already meets WCAG AA, which is the level most accessibility policies require. High contrast goes beyond that, and is worth turning on if you know your audience needs it or if your timeline will be read on projectors or in bright light.
